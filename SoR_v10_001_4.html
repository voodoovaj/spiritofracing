Spirit of Racing v1.0 · Arm Chair Development
What Is This?
Spirit of Racing is a single-file HTML motorsport career tracker. Race in the sim of your choice, enter the results here, and SoR turns them into a living championship — standings, qualifying grids, driver ratings, team dynamics, and an optional AI-driven career narrative full of emails, goals and paddock drama.

Everything runs in your browser. No account, no server, no installation. The AI narrative is entirely optional — switch it off and the whole game still works.
Getting Started
1. Open the HTML file in any modern browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari)
2. On the splash screen, select a season and click Enter Season
3. Go to the Career tab — set your Player Name and Driver Slot (the driver whose seat you occupy)
4. Go to Races, open Round 1, and hit Start ▶ to reveal the grid
5. Race it in your sim, come back, enter the finishing positions and hit Save Results

Your save lives in the browser's localStorage. Use ⬇ Export regularly to back it up as a JSON file.

Season Types
Set in the Roster editor under Race Settings. Each changes how a season is run:

Standard — a normal championship.
Street Mode — underground racing. No press, no stewards, no officials. A scene correspondent replaces the journalist.
Multiclass — two classes of five teams, racing together but scored separately. Enter your overall finishing position and SoR works out your class position. Standings show your class only.
One-Make — everyone in identical machinery. Enter the car name and hit Build grid to create one numbered entry per driver. Tier is ignored and there are no teammates.
Endurance — race length is measured in minutes rather than laps.
Your Driver Slot
You occupy one driver's seat in the roster. That driver becomes inactive — a 🔴 red dot in the team panel. They still exist in the paddock and may appear in the narrative, but they no longer score.

Your personal points are tracked separately and travel with you between teams. A team only gets constructor credit for what you scored while driving for them. Active teammates show a 🟢 green dot.
Races & Qualifying
Each round opens with a briefing card showing your goal and start position. Hit Start ▶ to reveal the grid. Once you've saved results you can return to it with ◀ Briefing.

The qualifying model builds each grid from:
• Where every driver finished last race — the main signal early in a season
• Where they sit in the championship — weighted more heavily as the season progresses, so title contenders start near the front for the finale even after a bad round
• Team strength, which pulls quick cars forward but never pushes slow ones back
• Driver rating, which sets how predictable each driver is — a top-rated driver lands near their expected slot, a poor one is scattered

Other rules: pole is a weighted lottery among the front runners, so nobody monopolises it. Whoever took pole last time is slightly harder to repeat it. Nobody starts in the same slot three races running, or last two races running. Teammates never end up more than a handful of rows apart, and the back row is never an all-one-team lockout. Occasionally a backmarker has a lucky session and qualifies in the top ten.

Penalties appear beside the affected driver as a red −N grid penalty, with the reason on hover.
Weather is rolled per round and leans on the season's narrative arc — the darkest stretch of a season is wetter than its opening.
⚡ Simulate Results auto-fills positions from the grid with noise, for rounds you want to skip through.
Driver Ratings
The Rating tab in Standings grades every driver on what they do with the car they have — comparing where they start to where they finish, across every season you have played.

Grades run F through A, with A+ for drivers who have finished top three in a completed championship. Ratings marked with * are provisional — fewer than five races of data.

Setting them by hand: the roster editor has a rating dropdown next to every driver. Leave it on Auto to let the game earn it, or set a grade directly to match a driver's real-world reputation. A manual grade applies immediately, overrides anything the game calculated, follows that driver across every season they appear in, and shows in gold so you can see at a glance which are hand-set.
Goals
A goal is set automatically the moment you save a round, with no API call. It is always a person — the driver directly ahead of you in the championship, shown with the points gap. Lead the championship and it becomes holding off whoever is closest behind. In the last rounds it becomes the finishing position that actually secures the title.

Your crew chief will reference it. Whether you chase it is up to you.
The Cast
Stefano — crew chief. Your campaign. How you drove, what the result did to the championship, what has to happen from here. Deep into a season, if the title is a formality or already gone, he may suggest adjusting the car — faster if you are out of it, slower if it is over. It is only ever a suggestion; the tier knob is yours.

Andrea — personal assistant. Everyone else. Feuds, sightings, contract manoeuvring, whose personal life is leaking into their driving. You are who Andrea writes to, not who Andrea writes about — you may not appear in the email at all, and that is the format working.

Press covers the championship from outside. Race Direction issues penalties. In Street Mode a scene correspondent replaces the press.

Each can be switched off individually in Settings → Cast. Switching one off removes both their voice from the prompt and their email from the batch, which lowers cost on both sides.
Relationships
Every driver has a relationship level with you, shown as an emoji in the standings. Click one to see the label and adjust it with ± buttons.

😈 Rival — hostile, pointed, never cordial
😤 Tense — clipped, guarded, competitive
😐 Neutral
🙂 Friendly — warmer than average
😊 Ally — genuine support, may share information

Relationships shift automatically based on what happens on track and carry between seasons — grudges fade but do not vanish. They are binding on the narrative: a Rival will never write you a friendly email.
Inbox
📬 Race report sits beside Save Results and lights up gold once the round is saved. It covers the race you just ran and looks ahead to the next. The same alert appears in the inbox as You have mail.

Reports are available after every round. Skip a few and the next one becomes a chapter covering everything since you last read — nothing is lost by ignoring it for a while.
Championship History
The History page permanently records every completed season — driver champion, constructor champion, the car they won in, and a running trophy count. Replaying a season adds a second entry rather than overwriting the first. Your wins are gold, AI wins red.
Season Management
Restart Season — zeros race results and grid locks, keeps career data. Also clears briefing flags and the championship record for that season, so a replay is recorded fresh.
Start Over — full wipe including personal scores, inbox, goals and narrative.
Rename — carries all associated data across with the new name.
Team Tiers — set in the roster editor, 1 = fastest, 10 = slowest. A wide spread produces more realistic grids. The number is internal: characters describe cars in words, never as a tier, so you can retune mid-season for competitive balance without it showing.
Saving Your Game
SoR uses browser localStorage, so data persists between sessions automatically.

⚠️ localStorage is browser and device specific. Clearing browser data will erase your save.

⬇ Export — downloads a dated JSON backup of everything.
⬆ Import — restores from a JSON file, replacing current data.

Recommended: export after every session, and keep a couple of recent backups.
Cost
None. The Race Engineer report, penalties, goals, ratings, weather, standings and grids are all calculated locally in your browser. There is nothing to pay for and no internet connection required.
Tips
🏎 Use a wide tier spread. Try 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10 rather than clustering — it produces grids with real midfield gaps.
📬 The Race Engineer report is always accurate. Start position, finish position and the next goal come straight from your save data — nothing is invented.
😈 Let relationships develop. They move on their own. Nudge them manually only if you want to make peace or escalate.
⭐ Set ratings for real drivers. A roster of real names feels far better when the grades match their reputations.
💾 Export before big changes. Before resetting, switching seasons or editing the roster.
